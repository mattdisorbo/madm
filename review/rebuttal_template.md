# Rebuttal Templates

Use one of these per reviewer. The examples show variety in surface form, but the successful ones all do the same thing: identify the bottleneck, answer it directly, and tie the answer to an exact revision, result, or new analysis.

## Template A: Concern-first

Thank you for the helpful comments. We addressed the main concerns about `[concern 1]` and `[concern 2]`.

1. `[Concern 1 short label]`  
We `[exact change]`. The updated `[table/figure/section]` now shows `[exact result]`. This addresses the concern because `[one-sentence reason]`.

2. `[Concern 2 short label]`  
We `[exact change]`. The new results show `[exact result]`. We also revised `[section/discussion]` to clarify `[interpretation]`.

We hope these additions resolve the main concerns.

Best for:

- added experiments
- statistical clarifications
- reviewer raises two clear objections

## Template B: Quote-and-answer

Thank you for the thoughtful review. We respond to the main points below.

> `[reviewer concern or question]`

`[direct answer in 2-4 sentences with exact revision or result]`

> `[reviewer concern or question]`

`[direct answer in 2-4 sentences with exact revision or result]`

Best for:

- multi-part reviews
- conceptual objections
- when the reviewer’s wording is precise and worth anchoring on

## Template C: Result-first

We made two changes in response to this review.

1. We `[added experiment or analysis]`. The result is `[exact finding]`, which resolves the concern about `[issue]`.
2. We `[revised framing, figure, or discussion]` to make clear that `[interpretation]`.

Best for:

- cases like `a_long_way_to_go`
- when the rebuttal’s main job is to show new evidence fast

## Template D: Clarification-first

Thank you for the helpful suggestion. The key point is `[one-sentence clarification of what the paper claims]`.

On `[issue 1]`, we now clarify `[definition or scope]` in `[section]`.  
On `[issue 2]`, we revised `[section]` to explain `[motivation or limitation]`.

Best for:

- definitional concerns
- framing issues
- cases like `dated_data` or `auxiliary_task_demands`

## Rules

- choose the template that matches the reviewer’s bottleneck
- keep it to two or three points unless the review really needs more
- every point must contain an exact change, exact result, or exact commitment to revision
- answer the reviewer in their order when possible
- do not restate the whole review
- do not flatter the reviewer or quote praise back to them
- do not add unrelated improvements
- close in one sentence at most

## What The Examples Suggest

- `dated_data.thread.md`: best venue-matched style for us; quote the concern, answer it directly, promise exact text or analysis changes
- `a_long_way_to_go.thread.md`: best example of score-moving rebuttal; lead with added experiments and new results
- `tuning_by_proxy.thread.md`: useful when a reviewer asks for mechanism or theory; add real analysis, not just prose
- `tree_of_thoughts.thread.md`: oral paper, but not a style template; stronger novelty carried more weight than rebuttal craft
